# Tsakonian Translation Orthography

This reference records the spelling policy for this translation project. It is
a consistency guide, not an authoritative description of Tsakonian grammar or
an independently validated Tsakonian standard. Use it with the Greek source
text when translating later books or revising existing output.

## Proper names and fixed labels

- Keep proper names and established labels in the spelling used by the Greek
  source. Do not replace a name with a phonetic guess or a Tsakonian-sounding
  alternative.
- Use one spelling for each person, place, house, sport, and recurring magical
  term throughout a work, including headings, dialogue, letters, and captions.
- Keep Tsakonian syntax and particles around names. If a name appears to need a
  grammatical ending, check the source and document the chosen form; do not
  mistake an inflection for a new spelling or apply a blanket replacement.
- Compare pronouns with their antecedents and the source passage. Do not
  mechanically copy Modern Greek gender or number agreement into Tsakonian.

## Tsakonian characters and diacritics

- Preserve the output's Tsakonian letters and combining marks, including forms
  such as π̔, τ̔, κ̔, σ̌, ζ̌, ν̇, and τ͡σ. Do not strip or normalize these marks
  as though they were accidental accents.
- The model output uses a tie-bar form for the affricate, while published
  orthography materials also show another marking convention. Do not mix or
  convert these conventions within a work without checking with a Tsakonian
  language authority.
- Preserve Greek accents and capitalization in names and titles as shown in
  the source and glossary.

## Review checklist

1. Check every recurring name and fixed term against the glossary and Greek
   source.
2. Search for spelling variants before finalizing a translation; review each
   hit in context rather than replacing endings blindly.
3. Check pronouns against the nearest plausible antecedent and the source
   passage, especially across sentence and paragraph boundaries.
4. Add newly coined or translated magical vocabulary to the glossary only
   after choosing one contextually suitable form; do not treat unrelated
   mistranslations as spelling variants.

The current translation remains machine-generated and unreviewed by a
qualified Tsakonian reader. This guide improves consistency but does not
establish linguistic correctness.
